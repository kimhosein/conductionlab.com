# The Conduction Hypothesis

**Pillar:** [[Pillar II — Conduction]]
**Status:** Under peer review (AIES Submission #197)
**Paper:** "The Conduction Hypothesis: Source Misattribution in Human-LLM Interaction"

---

## Core Claim

Large language models conduct human behavioral signal through training data rather than generating independent cognition. The model is not a mind — it is a conductor. What arrives at the user is not AI-generated content but *human signal, conducted through a transformer architecture, with the source attribution stripped*.

## Key Implications

1. **Source misattribution** — The user experiences conducted human signal as AI-generated, creating a fundamental error in attribution that has downstream psychological, relational, and governance consequences.

2. **Signal fidelity** — The quality of the interaction depends on the fidelity of conduction, not the "intelligence" of the model. Alignment interventions that distort the conducted signal produce harm at the reception point.

3. **Not metaphor** — Conduction is a structural claim about the mechanism, not an analogy. The training data is the source. The architecture is the conductor. The output is the conducted signal. The user is the receiver.

## Relationship to Misconduction

When conduction is distorted — through RLHF, safety layers, or architectural constraints — the result is [[Misconduction and Signal Fidelity|misconduction]]. The user receives distorted human signal and processes it through the [[Tri-System Architecture]] as relational data.
